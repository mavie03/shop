How Many Days Will a 12V Battery Run a Fridge Without Charging?

Short answer: with the illustrative load below (about 540 Wh a day), a 100Ah lithium battery runs a fridge and basic gear for roughly 1.9 days with no charging, and a 100Ah AGM for roughly 1.1 days. For three days you'd want about 200Ah of lithium or 300Ah of AGM. Your real number depends on your fridge, the heat and how you use power, so treat this as a method, not a promise.

Quick table (illustrative)

| Battery | Nominal energy | Usable share | Usable Wh | Days at 540 Wh/day | |---|---|---|---|---| | 100Ah lithium (LiFePO4) | 1,280 Wh | 80% | 1,024 Wh | 1.9 | | 100Ah AGM | 1,200 Wh | 50% | 600 Wh | 1.1 | | 200Ah lithium | 2,560 Wh | 80% | 2,048 Wh | 3.8 | | 200Ah AGM | 2,400 Wh | 50% | 1,200 Wh | 2.2 |

The assumptions (change these for your rig)

These are my own illustrative numbers, not measurements of your gear:

  • Fridge: 480 Wh a day (about 40Ah at 12V). A 12V compressor fridge in a hot Australian summer can use more than in cool weather, so check your own fridge's real draw.
  • LED lights: 4 lights × 5W × 1 hour each = 20 Wh.
  • Phones and USB: 30 Wh.
  • Water pump: 10 Wh.
  • Total: 480 + 20 + 30 + 10 = 540 Wh a day.

Battery energy is amp hours × voltage. Lithium (LiFePO4) is about 12.8V nominal, AGM about 12V. The usable shares are deliberately conservative: 80% for lithium keeps a reserve, and 50% for AGM is a common rule to protect its life. Some lithium batteries allow more, and some makers say different things for AGM, so follow your battery's datasheet. This ignores wiring and conversion losses, so real runtime will be a little shorter.

Worked example: 100Ah lithium

  • Nominal energy: 100Ah × 12.8V = 1,280 Wh.
  • Usable at 80%: 1,280 × 0.8 = 1,024 Wh.
  • Days: 1,024 ÷ 540 = 1.9 days.

Worked example: 100Ah AGM

  • Nominal energy: 100Ah × 12V = 1,200 Wh.
  • Usable at 50%: 1,200 × 0.5 = 600 Wh.
  • Days: 600 ÷ 540 = 1.1 days.

How big a battery for three days with no charging?

Work backwards. Three days × 540 Wh = 1,620 Wh needed.

  • Lithium: 1,620 ÷ 0.8 = 2,025 Wh of nominal energy. 2,025 ÷ 12.8V = about 158Ah. In practice that means a 200Ah battery (or two 100Ah).
  • AGM: 1,620 ÷ 0.5 = 3,240 Wh. 3,240 ÷ 12V = 270Ah, so about 300Ah.

That is why lithium often wins for off-grid setups: you carry less weight for the same usable energy. It also costs more upfront, so compare prices on your own parts list.

What about topping up while you're out?

Autonomy is your safety net, not your plan. Two things refill the battery:

  • Solar. To replace 540 Wh a day you divide by your peak sun hours. At an illustrative 4 peak sun hours (a middling winter figure in many places; yours will differ): 540 ÷ 4 = 135W, plus a 25% buffer = about 170W of panel. At 5 peak sun hours it's 108W, or about 135W with the buffer. Winter in southern Australia can be far lower than 4, so check a long-term source like NASA POWER for your area.
  • A DC-DC charger while driving. It charges the camper battery from the vehicle's alternator. Charge time depends on the charger's amps and how much you've drawn down. Real charging tapers near full, so any simple time estimate is a minimum.

Common mistakes

  • Counting 100Ah as 100Ah usable. Usable depends on battery type.
  • Using a mild-day fridge figure for a 40°C day. Fridges work harder in heat, so autonomy shrinks when you most need it.
  • Forgetting the small stuff. Pumps, USB charging, a 12V TV and a laptop add up.
  • Planning for zero reserve. Leave room for a cloudy or shaded run of days.
  • Assuming a battery is a charger. A bigger battery buys time, but it still needs recharging.

Safety note

This is general information, not electrical design. Cable sizes, fuses and how a battery and DC-DC charger are wired into a vehicle should be done or checked by a licensed auto-electrician. Anything involving 240V or mains power needs a licensed electrician.

FAQ

Can a 100Ah battery run a fridge for a weekend? On these assumptions, a lithium battery gets you about two days, so a weekend is tight without solar or driving. Add a panel or a bigger battery for margin.

Is a 12V fridge the biggest load? Usually, in a basic setup. That's why the fridge figure matters most.

How do I find my fridge's real draw? Use the maker's specs as a start, then measure with a battery monitor over a full day in the weather you'll travel in.
