12V Power Setup Checklist: What to Work Out Before You Buy

Short answer: before you buy anything, work out five numbers in this order: daily watt-hours, battery size, solar watts, DC-DC charger amps, and (with a licensed auto-electrician) cables and fuses. Everything else follows from the first number. Most expensive mistakes come from buying the battery first and guessing the rest.

| Step | What you work out | Rough method (illustrative) | |---|---|---| | 1 | Daily watt-hours (Wh) | Watts x hours used, for every device, added up | | 2 | Battery capacity | Daily Wh x days without charging, divided by usable % and voltage | | 3 | Solar watts | Daily Wh / peak sun hours, plus a buffer | | 4 | DC-DC charger amps | Match to battery size and vehicle; confirm with the maker | | 5 | Cables and fuses | Licensed auto-electrician |

Step 1: Daily watt-hours

List every 12V item, its watts, and the hours it runs per day. Multiply and add.

Example (all figures are assumptions for illustration):

  • Compressor fridge: 480 Wh/day. This is a typical value; it varies a lot with the fridge, the weather and how often you open it.
  • Three LED lights, 5 W each, 4 hours: 3 x 5 x 4 = 60 Wh
  • Water pump, 60 W for 10 minutes (1/6 hour): 10 Wh
  • Phone and device charging: 60 Wh

Total: 480 + 60 + 10 + 60 = 610 Wh per day.

Step 2: Battery size

Decide how many days you want to run with little or no charging. Say 2 days: 610 x 2 = 1,220 Wh.

Then allow for usable capacity (depth of discharge). Common rules of thumb are about 50% for AGM and about 80% for lithium. These vary by maker, so check your battery's datasheet.

  • Lithium (12.8 V nominal): 1,220 / 0.8 = 1,525 Wh; 1,525 / 12.8 = 119 Ah, so roughly a 120 Ah battery.
  • AGM (12 V nominal): 1,220 / 0.5 = 2,440 Wh; 2,440 / 12 = 203 Ah, so roughly 200 Ah or more.

That gap is why the same trip can need very different batteries. Weight, price and charging behaviour differ too.

Step 3: Solar

A common formula: daily Wh / peak sun hours, then add a buffer of 20 to 30% for losses, shade and dust. Peak sun hours are the big unknown, so treat both values below as illustrative.

  • Summer-ish, 4.5 peak sun hours: 610 / 4.5 = 136 W; with a 25% buffer, 136 x 1.25 = about 170 W.
  • Winter-ish, 3 peak sun hours: 610 / 3 = 203 W; x 1.25 = about 254 W.

Size for the season you will actually camp in. Many guides ignore winter, and the shortfall is where flat batteries come from. Tilt, shade, cloud and red dust all change real output.

Step 4: DC-DC charger

A DC-DC charger charges your house battery from the vehicle's alternator, and handles the voltage differences and protects the starter battery. Retailer rules of thumb often pair roughly 100 to 150 Ah of battery with a 25 A charger, and 200 to 300 Ah with 40 A. Sources differ, and some use a percentage of battery Ah instead, so check the maker's recommendation.

Charge time example for the 120 Ah lithium battery with a 25 A charger, charging from 20% to 100% (that is 80% of 120 = 96 Ah): 96 / 25 = 3.8 hours at best. Real charging tapers near full, and the actual output also depends on your alternator and vehicle wiring, so treat this as a minimum.

Step 5: Cables and fuses

Cable thickness and fuse ratings depend on current, run length and voltage drop. This is where safety matters, so have a licensed auto-electrician specify and fit them. Anything 240V or mains needs a licensed electrician.

Common mistakes

  • Buying parts before totalling the Wh. Without a number you can't judge any quote.
  • Using the fridge's label watts. A compressor cycles on and off; daily Wh is what counts.
  • Sizing solar for summer only. Check the darkest month you will travel in.
  • Treating 100 Ah as 100 Ah usable. Depth of discharge changes the answer.
  • Forgetting a margin. Add a buffer for ageing, dust and cloudy days.
  • Ignoring the vehicle. The charger's real output depends on the alternator and wiring.

Before-you-buy checklist

  • [ ] Every device listed with watts and hours
  • [ ] Daily Wh total, with a buffer
  • [ ] Days of autonomy chosen
  • [ ] Battery type and usable % checked against the datasheet
  • [ ] Solar sized for your travel season and region
  • [ ] DC-DC amps compared with the maker's guidance
  • [ ] Cables and fuses quoted by a licensed auto-electrician
  • [ ] Prices compared across at least three retailers
