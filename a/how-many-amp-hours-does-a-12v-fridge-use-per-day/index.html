How Many Amp Hours Does a 12V Fridge Use Per Day?

The fridge is usually the biggest, most constant load in a camper trailer, caravan or 4WD. Get its daily use right and the rest of your 12V sizing gets much easier. This guide shows how to estimate it, then turn it into battery and solar numbers.

The short answer

A typical 40-50 litre 12V compressor fridge uses somewhere around 30 to 50 amp hours (Ah) per day in Australian touring conditions. That's an estimate, not a measurement. Your figure depends on the fridge, the outside temperature, how often you open it, and how full it is.

Step 1: Find the running watts

Look at the fridge's spec sheet or compliance label for its running power. Many 40-50L models quote something in the 40-60 W range while the compressor is running. For this example we'll assume 45 W (an assumption, so swap in your own).

At 12 V, current is watts divided by volts:

45 W ÷ 12 V = 3.75 A while the compressor is running.

Step 2: Estimate how long it runs (duty cycle)

The compressor cycles on and off. The share of time it's running is the duty cycle. Rough planning assumptions (estimates only, so log your own if you can):

  • Mild weather, fridge in the shade: about 33% (8 hours in 24)
  • Hot weather, fridge in a warm vehicle or full of warm drinks: about 50% (12 hours in 24)

Step 3: Calculate daily watt-hours

Daily watt-hours = running watts × 24 hours × duty cycle.

  • Mild: 45 × 24 × 0.33 = about 356 Wh/day
  • Hot: 45 × 24 × 0.50 = 540 Wh/day

To convert to amp hours at 12 V, divide by 12:

  • Mild: 356 ÷ 12 = about 30 Ah/day
  • Hot: 540 ÷ 12 = 45 Ah/day

That's where the 30-50Ah range comes from. Plan on the hot figure if you travel through the Top End, the Red Centre or anywhere in summer. It's the safer number.

Tip: the best test is your own. Fully charge the battery, run only the fridge for 24 hours, and see how much the battery monitor says you used.

Step 4: Turn it into battery size

Say you want the fridge to run for 2 days with no sun or driving (a common planning choice). Using the hot figure:

540 Wh × 2 days = 1,080 Wh of usable energy.

You can't use all of a battery's rated capacity, so divide by the usable fraction. These fractions are common rules of thumb. Check your battery's datasheet.

  • Lithium (LiFePO4), assume 80% usable: 1,080 ÷ 0.8 = 1,350 Wh. At about 12.8 V nominal, that's 1,350 ÷ 12.8 = about 105 Ah. A 100-120Ah battery is the sensible range.
  • AGM, assume 50% usable: 1,080 ÷ 0.5 = 2,160 Wh. At 12 V that's 2,160 ÷ 12 = 180 Ah.

This is fridge only. Add lights, a water pump, phone charging and so on before choosing a battery.

Step 5: Turn it into solar size

Solar output depends on peak sun hours (PSH): the equivalent hours per day of full-strength sun. Real figures vary by location, season, tilt, shade and dust. The values below are illustrative only.

Also allow for losses in wiring and the charge controller. We'll assume 75% of panel rating actually reaches the battery.

Panel watts = daily Wh ÷ (PSH × 0.75)

  • At 4 PSH (a cautious winter-type figure): 540 ÷ (4 × 0.75) = 540 ÷ 3 = 180 W
  • At 5 PSH: 540 ÷ (5 × 0.75) = 540 ÷ 3.75 = 144 W

Check: 180 W × 4 h = 720 Wh, and 720 × 0.75 = 540 Wh. That matches the fridge's daily need.

Remember the panel also has to cover everything else you run, so most people go bigger than the fridge alone needs.

Step 6: What about the DC-DC charger?

While driving, a DC-DC charger tops up the second battery from the alternator. For a fridge-only setup, even a modest unit can replace a day's use over a few hours of driving, but the right size depends on your battery's charge limits and everything else you run. The charger's size also drives cable and fuse choices. Those need a licensed auto-electrician, so don't guess them.

Common mistakes

  • Using the fridge's label watts as if it runs all day. That overestimates, though it's safer than underestimating.
  • Forgetting heat. A fridge in a hot car or a poorly ventilated drawer runs far more.
  • Mixing up Wh and Ah. Ah only makes sense with a voltage. Convert with Wh = Ah × V.
  • Counting 100% of battery capacity as usable.
  • Sizing for the best sun day. Size for the worst month you'll camp in.

Safety

Wiring, fusing and cable sizing for 12V systems carry fire and shock risks. Have a licensed auto-electrician check or install the system. Anything 240V or mains must be done by a licensed electrician.
